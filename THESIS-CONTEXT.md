## Research question and hypotheses

Title (approved by Husnu 11 Aug 2026, "The title is good."): **"The Dollar's Safe-Haven Status Across Two Shocks: Evidence from Liberation Day and the Strait of Hormuz Crisis"**.

Research question as proposed to Husnu (19 May 2026 email, verbatim): "does the US dollar still function as a safe-haven currency, and does its response differ between a trade-policy shock and a military and oil-supply shock?" The canonical phrasing inside `content/01_introduction.tex` has not been re-verified in the ch.5 sessions; check it there before quoting "the" research question in another chapter.

Prediction: it holds in a military/oil-supply shock (Hormuz, and the Ukraine external benchmark) and weakens or inverts in a trade-policy shock that originates in US policy (Liberation Day). ## Variable definitions and notation

All symbols as they appear in `content/05_data_methodology.tex` (the chapter defines each at first use — keep any new chapter consistent with these):

- $S_{i,t}$ — spot rate of currency $i$ on day $t$, foreign currency per dollar.
- $r_{i,t}=\ln(S_{i,t-1}/S_{i,t})$ — daily log return; **positive = appreciation of the foreign currency against the dollar**.
- $r^{\mathrm{EUR}}_{X,t} = r^{\mathrm{USD}}_{X,t} - r_{\mathrm{EUR},t}$ — euro-denominated commodity return, $X \in$ {Brent, gold}, $r_{\mathrm{EUR},t}$ = euro's log appreciation vs the dollar.
- $f_{i,t}-s_{i,t}$ — one-month forward discount, log forward minus log spot; proxies the interest differential under covered interest parity.
- $\AR_{i,t} = R_{i,t} - \hat\mu_i$, with $\hat\mu_i = \frac{1}{120}\sum_{s=-140}^{-21} R_{i,s}$ — abnormal return under the constant-mean model.
- $\CAR_i(t_1,t_2) = \sum_{t=t_1}^{t_2} \AR_{i,t}$.
- $t = \CAR_i(t_1,t_2) / (\hat\sigma_i \sqrt{L})$, $L = t_2-t_1+1$, $\hat\sigma_i$ = estimation-window std of $\AR_{i,t}$.
- $\Delta = \CAR_A - \CAR_B$, $\mathrm{s.e.}(\Delta) = \sqrt{L(\hat\sigma_A^2+\hat\sigma_B^2)}$ — cross-event difference test (two-sample analogue of the CAR variance in Campbell–Lo–MacKinlay).
- Market model (robustness): $R_{i,t} = \alpha_i + \beta_i R_{m,t} + \varepsilon_{i,t}$, $R_{m,t}$ = the equal-weighted dollar basket.
- Regression (§5.3): $r^{\text{USD}}_{t} = a + b_1\,\Delta R_t + \sum_{k}\beta_k(\Delta R_t \times D^{k}_t) + \sum_{k}\gamma_k D^{k}_t + \varepsilon_t$, $k\in\{\text{tariff},\,\text{Hormuz},\,\text{Ukraine}\}$; $D^k_t$ = 1 from event day through 20 trading days after; $\gamma_k$ absorb the average dollar drift within each window; $\Delta R_t$ standardized to unit variance over the sample.
- The `\AR` and `\CAR` macros exist in the preamble; the $t$ of the t-test is set in math italics (checked and kept).

## Specification decisions

Each with its reason; these were argued once and should not be re-argued:

- **Constant-mean return model as the main event-study specification** — the object of study is the dollar's common movement; a market model would subtract the common movement, the very thing being measured. Kwok–Brooks (1990) find currency event-study inference sensitive to numeraire and benchmark model and prefer mean-adjusted returns when the whole unexpected movement is the quantity of interest.
- **Market model kept as robustness only**, $R_{m,t}$ = equal-weighted dollar basket; basket-level results in Appendix A. Hierarchy claim deliberately qualified: "The safe-haven ordering under both treatment shocks is unchanged" (true at (0,5) for all four events; at (0,20) the Ukraine ordering flips and the 12-day war is n.s. — hence "both treatment shocks", not "all events"). This exact string is in `content/tab_market_model.tex` AND its generator `Code/make_mm_table.py` so regeneration keeps it.
- **Event window (−20,+20) trading days; estimation window (−140,−21)** (120 days), ending where the event window begins so no event-window day enters the benchmark. Long-horizon persistence check CAR(0,+50) uses estimation (−170,−51) so it stays clear of its wider (−50,+50) event window (`04_event_study_w50.py`).
- **CAR windows (−1,+1), (0,+1), (0,+5) are the headline; (0,+10), (0,+20) are robustness/persistence** — per Husnu (17 Jul 2026): "20 days would be too long for financial markets, many things happen at the same time, but it'd be good for a robustness test." The longer the window, the weaker the attribution claim (MacKinlay, Kothari–Warner).
- **Day 0 = first trading day the market can react**; announcements on non-trading days shift forward (Hormuz: Sat 2026-02-28 → Mon 2026-03-02). Liberation Day and the tariff pause came in the U.S. afternoon (just after 4 p.m. ET on 2 Apr 2025, cited to Benguria–Saffie 2025; early afternoon 9 Apr 2025, uncited by choice), after the H.10 noon pricing and late in the currency panel's pricing day, so day-0 contains at most a first partial reaction — the standard reason for the two-day window.
- **Plain t-test, no Kolari–Pynnönen adjustment** — cross-sectional dependence inflation applies when many correlated securities share a common event date; here inference is at the basket level around single, non-overlapping event dates, and the basket σ̂ already embodies member correlation (no common-date averaging across securities).
- **Two events treated as independent in the Δ test** because their windows do not overlap (verified exactly, see events table).
- **Baskets, main sort**: terciles of 23 currencies by average one-month forward discount over the sample → safe = low-yield seven, risky = high-yield seven. Pegged/managed (DKK, HKD, SAR, KWD, CNY) excluded — cannot move on risk news. NDF-only (BRL, CLP, COP, PEN) excluded from the sort — no clean 1M quote. Terciles not quintiles: sizing convention of the sorting literature (AMP sort 10 currencies into three equal groups; LRV form six portfolios from at most 35); quintiles would leave four or five per basket, few enough for one currency's swings to dominate. **Corrected 2026-09-14:** this fixed tercile classification underlies the event-study baskets ONLY. The monthly DOL/CARRY factors that `build_fx_factors.py` also produces use quintiles re-sorted each month (N_PORT = 5) and feed NO reported result since Fig 5.1 was dropped on 2026-08-04; the §5.1.2/§5.1.3 sentences that describe the monthly factors and said "This is the classification that underlies the carry factor" and the intro's "rebuilt carry/dollar risk factors" contribution were wrong and were corrected 2026-09-15 (batch item 5.7). Keep the code (it writes `carry_classification.csv`).
- **Named baskets as second scheme (robustness)**: JPY, CHF, EUR safe (Ranaldo–Söderlind) vs TRY, BRL, ZAR, MXN, COP, IDR risky (De Bock–de Carvalho Filho). Euro enters as the largest liquid alternative to the dollar, not as a documented haven (haven evidence pre-2008 only, Habib–Stracca; euro as principal alternative, Jiang–Krishnamurthy–Lustig–Richmond 2026).
- **Oil split**: net crude trade 2019–2024 (UN Comtrade HS 2709, in-repo data): exporters NOK, CAD, MXN, COP, BRL, SAR, KWD vs importers JPY, KRW, INR, THB, TWD, TRY. Chen–Liu–Wang–Zhu (2016, p.1038) apply the same split to the six of these in their sample; mechanism = terms of trade (Chen–Rogoff 2003).
- **All baskets equally weighted** (sorting-literature convention); dollar basket = EW average of the dollar's return against all 32 currencies.
